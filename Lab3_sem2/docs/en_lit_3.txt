Title: Essay: Irony in Narrative Fiction
Language: english
Domain: literature
ID: en_lit_3

Irony creates a gap between appearance and reality that readers must actively interpret. Verbal
irony occurs when stated meaning diverges from intended meaning. Situational irony arises when
events contradict reasonable expectations. Dramatic irony places the audience in a position of
superior knowledge relative to characters.

Novelists use irony to critique social norms without direct moralizing. Free indirect style can
blend a character's voice with narratorial distance, producing subtle ironic effects. Satirical
traditions weaponize irony against hypocrisy and excess. Yet excessive irony risks emotional
detachment and cynicism.

In contemporary fiction, irony often coexists with sincerity rather than replacing it. Authors
may undercut a romantic plot while still affirming the value of connection. Readers negotiate
these layered tones by attending to diction, pacing and structural echoes. Irony thus remains a
flexible instrument for exploring contradiction in human experience and public life.
